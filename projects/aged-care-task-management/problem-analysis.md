# Problem & Root Cause Analysis

## Aged Care Daily & Weekly Task Management

**Project Type:** Business Process Improvement
**Role:** Business Analyst

---

## 1. Problem Statement

The current process for communicating and monitoring daily and weekly tasks relies heavily on manual communication.

Tasks may be communicated verbally or through informal channels, which can make it difficult for staff and clinical leaders to maintain a consistent view of what needs to be done, who is responsible, and what has already been completed.

The problem is not simply the lack of automation. The underlying issue is that there is no consistent process for managing task ownership, status and follow-up in one place.

---

## 2. Business Impact

The current process can create several operational challenges:

### Communication

Staff may receive task instructions through different communication methods.

This can make it difficult to maintain a consistent record of instructions.

### Visibility

There may be limited visibility of outstanding tasks across the team.

### Follow-Up

RNs and CCCs may need to manually follow up with staff to determine whether tasks have been completed.

### Recurring Tasks

Daily and weekly activities may require repeated communication rather than being managed through a consistent recurring-task process.

### Accountability

Without clear task ownership and status, it can be harder to identify who is responsible for an outstanding task.

# 4. Root Cause Analysis

The analysis suggests that several of the visible problems have a common underlying cause.

### Immediate Problem

Tasks are communicated and monitored manually.

↓

### Contributing Factors

* No central task list
* No consistent task ownership
* No standard status process
* Limited visibility of task completion
* No structured exception workflow
* Recurring tasks require repeated coordination

↓

### Underlying Root Cause

**The current process does not provide a consistent, centralised method for assigning, tracking and monitoring operational tasks.**

---

# 5. Five Whys Analysis

## Problem

**Why can it be difficult to know whether a task has been completed?**

### Why 1

Because task completion may need to be communicated or checked manually.

### Why 2

Because staff do not have a single shared task-status view.

### Why 3

Because tasks are not consistently managed through one central process.

### Why 4

Because the current workflow relies on manual communication and follow-up.

### Why 5

Because there is no standardised task-management approach covering assignment, status, completion and exceptions.

### Root Cause

**The organisation lacks a consistent central process for managing operational task ownership and status.**

---

# 6. Improvement Opportunities

Based on the analysis, the main improvement opportunities are:

### 1. Central Task Visibility

Provide one place where staff can view assigned tasks and task status.

### 2. Clear Task Ownership

Make responsibility for each task clear.

### 3. Standardised Status

Use consistent statuses such as:

* Not Started
* In Progress
* Completed
* Overdue
* Exception

### 4. Recurring Task Management

Allow appropriate daily and weekly tasks to be generated according to predefined schedules.

### 5. Exception Management

Provide a structured way for staff to record why a task could not be completed.

### 6. Clinical Oversight

Allow authorised RNs and CCCs to monitor relevant outstanding and overdue tasks.

---

# 7. Business Analysis Insight

The analysis shows why simply introducing a digital tool would not necessarily solve the problem.

The underlying process should be understood first.

The proposed solution therefore focuses on improving:

**Process → Ownership → Visibility → Status → Follow-Up**

rather than simply replacing verbal communication with software.

---

# 8. Assumptions

This analysis is based on a simulated portfolio case study and assumes that:

* The current process involves manual communication.
* Staff perform recurring operational tasks.
* RNs and CCCs require visibility of relevant task status.
* Task completion needs to be communicated or recorded.
* A digital task-management solution could potentially support the improved process.

These assumptions would need to be validated with stakeholders in a real project.
